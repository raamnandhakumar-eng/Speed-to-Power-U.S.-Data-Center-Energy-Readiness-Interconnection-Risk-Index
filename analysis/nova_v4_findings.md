# Northern Virginia V4 Findings

**As of September 21, 2026**

## Scope

V4 moves from transmission-project maturity to candidate development-pathway evidence.

It does not claim that any listed area has unused power capacity or an available development parcel.

The core records are:

- Campus A / Twin Creeks public precedent
- Campus B / Sycolin Creek + Starlight public precedent
- Campus C / Lunar + Apollo public precedent
- BECO-Firehouse future corridor evidence
- Global Plaza future corridor evidence

## 1. Why V4 separates technical pathway from land-use entitlement

Loudoun County changed the development environment materially.

On March 18, 2025, the county made data centers a conditional use requiring Special Exception approval in Industrial Park, General Industry, and Mineral Resources-Heavy Industry districts, subject to applicable grandfathering rules.

On September 15, 2026, the Board approved a plan to pause final votes on legislative data-center and electrical-substation applications for up to 12 months. As of the V4 analysis date, the implementing resolution was scheduled for October 20, 2026.

The Board stated that administrative applications would not be affected by the pause plan.

V4 therefore does not combine transmission maturity and zoning into one score.

A technically mature power pathway can still face a land-use timing gate.

## 2. Loudoun is actively changing both data-center and transmission policy

Data Center Standards & Locations Phase 2 is underway and is expected to move through Planning Commission and Board consideration in 2027.

The Electrical Infrastructure CPAM Phase 1 was adopted in February 2026. It supports colocation or expansion of existing transmission corridors as the county's first preference.

Phase 2 is evaluating potential future areas, zones, and routes for high-voltage infrastructure.

This creates policy uncertainty for greenfield candidate sites that is separate from utility queue readiness.

## 3. Campus A is a strong 300 MW precedent but not a 500 MW precedent

The Twin Creeks pathway documents:

- 300 MW requested load
- one delivery point
- a 2026 filing-era target
- 30 MVA of bridging capability from Pleasant View
- the approved Twin Creeks-Apollo 230 kV project

For a 300 MW scenario, the public precedent aligns with Dominion's 300 MW delivery-point cap.

For a 500 MW scenario, the same precedent only represents 60% of the target load and one of the minimum two required delivery-point requests.

V4 therefore reduces its scenario-fit score automatically.

## 4. Campus B is the clearest public multi-substation precedent

Campus B combines:

- Sycolin Creek: 300 MW
- Starlight: 255 MW
- total documented load: 555 MW
- two delivery points
- filing-era target sequence from 2026 through 2028

The source states that no bridging power was available for Customer B.

For a 500 MW scenario, Campus B provides the strongest public example of a two-substation pathway that exceeds the modeled campus requirement.

The score does not imply that the Campus B sites are available for another project.

It is a development precedent.

## 5. Campus C is another 500 MW-scale precedent with limited early-service evidence

Campus C combines:

- Lunar: 278 MW
- Apollo: 239 MW
- total documented load: 517 MW
- two delivery points
- 2028 filing-era target dates

The filing identifies bridging capability from Edwards Ferry.

V4 records the 30 MVA reference without assuming that it can be doubled or aggregated across both substations.

This is important because temporary bridging capability is small relative to a 500 MW ultimate campus requirement.

## 6. Firehouse is corridor evidence, not yet a scored candidate-site pathway

The BECO-Firehouse project would add two new 230 kV lines between existing BECO and a new Firehouse Substation.

Dominion filed the SCC application in May 2026 and expects service by early 2029.

The public source set does not identify a candidate data-center parcel, a requested campus MW value, or a delivery-point request tied to the V4 scenario.

V4 therefore reports the corridor's transmission maturity but does not manufacture a site score.

## 7. Global Plaza is reinforcement evidence with even less site-specific information

Global Plaza is a proposed double-circuit 230 kV line connecting two existing transmission lines.

Dominion states that no new substation is required in the current project scope and targets service by the end of 2029.

That may improve regional transmission conditions, but it does not establish a serving-substation pathway for a hypothetical site.

V4 therefore leaves the technical pathway score blank.

## 8. Land-use timing is now a first-class variable

Loudoun reports that data centers with demand of 100 MW or more may require multiple substations.

The county also reports 95 existing and proposed substations as of March 1, 2026.

At the same time, new data-center and substation approvals have become more procedurally constrained.

A real candidate-site model must therefore ask both:

1. Is there a plausible technical path to the requested megawatts?
2. Can the site and required electrical infrastructure secure the necessary land-use approvals on the required schedule?

## 9. Technical Pathway Evidence Score

For public campus precedents, V4 uses:

- 30% documented load fit
- 15% delivery-point fit
- 25% direct transmission-project maturity
- 20% schedule alignment
- 5% bridging-power ratio
- 5% evidence completeness

The score is scenario-specific.

Changing from 300 MW to 500 MW can materially change which public precedent is most analogous.

## 10. What V4 still does not know

V4 does not know:

- spare substation capacity
- transformer availability
- thermal line headroom
- contingency performance
- actual queue position for a hypothetical customer
- network-upgrade cost
- customer-funded transmission scope
- parcel-level zoning status
- land control
- water availability
- fiber availability
- gas availability
- actual construction lead times for a new customer

These become the requirements for V5 or a real project diligence process.
