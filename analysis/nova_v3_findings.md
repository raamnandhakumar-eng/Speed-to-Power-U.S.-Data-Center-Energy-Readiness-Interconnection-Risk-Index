# Northern Virginia V3 Findings

**As of September 21, 2026**

## Scope

V3 moves Speed-to-Power from utility territory to documented Northern Virginia transmission projects and load-serving substations.

The model does **not** estimate unused substation capacity.

It measures the maturity of publicly documented transmission development.

## 1. Dominion's queue is much larger than the existing system peak

Dominion's February 2026 filing states that:

- approximately **25,000 MW** of large-load requests had projected connection dates through December 31, 2031;
- another approximately **45,000 MW** had been assigned to study batches;
- approximately **70,000 MW** were advancing through the queue in total;
- the Dominion Zone all-time peak cited in the filing was **24,678 MW**;
- Dominion was receiving approximately **10 new large-load requests per month**, representing another **2,000–3,000 MW per month** of requested load.

The 70 GW queue figure is a request pipeline, not a forecast of realized load.

## 2. The formal queue creates a real site-readiness screen

The filing applies the standards to data-center delivery-point requests of approximately **100 MW or more**.

Each delivery-point request is capped at **300 MW**.

The four stages are:

1. Project Initiation
2. Project Feasibility
3. Project Development
4. Project Execution

Initial viability includes:

- sufficient land;
- constructible interconnection routes; and
- acceptable environmental conditions.

To advance through feasibility, the customer must provide a zoning conformance letter and a 30% engineering site plan.

Development requires additional permitting, a 100% grading plan, and a construction one-line diagram.

This makes site readiness a measurable part of the path to power rather than a qualitative side issue.

## 3. A 500 MW campus cannot be represented by one 500 MW delivery-point request

Because the public standards cap each delivery-point request at 300 MW:

- 100 MW scenario -> minimum **1** delivery point
- 300 MW scenario -> minimum **1** delivery point
- 500 MW scenario -> minimum **2** delivery points

Dominion's filing also states that campus-style developments with multiple substations on contiguous land are aligned and subject to staged advancement tied to demonstrated load-ramp thresholds.

## 4. Twin Creeks-to-Apollo provides unusually detailed public load evidence

The 2024 application identified five substations totaling **1,372 MW** of requested ten-year load:

| Substation | Requested load | Filing-era target in service | Bridging power |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Twin Creeks | 300 MW | June 2026 | Yes, Pleasant View 30 MVA |
| Sycolin Creek | 300 MW | September 2026 | No |
| Starlight | 255 MW | June 2028 | No |
| Lunar | 278 MW | February 2028 | Yes, Edwards Ferry 30 MVA |
| Apollo | 239 MW | March 2028 | Yes, Edwards Ferry 30 MVA |

These are filing-era requested loads and schedules, not current guaranteed service commitments.

For Twin Creeks and Sycolin Creek, the target dates have reached or approached the V3 analysis date. V3 does not infer actual completion without a separate current-status source.

## 5. The 500 kV reinforcement sequence is becoming physically mature

### Wishing Star-Mars

The project is the most mature major reinforcement in the V3 set. Dominion reports 500 kV GIS energization in January 2026, temporary bypass energization in February 2026, and construction completion in May 2026.

**TDRI: 95.5**

### Aspen-Golden

The SCC issued its final order in February 2025. Dominion's current project page gives January 2027 as the planned construction start and June 2028 as the SCC-approved in-service date.

**TDRI: 79.0**

### Golden-Mars

The project application was filed in March 2025 and the SCC issued its final order on June 29, 2026 approving Route 3A.

**TDRI: 76.0**

Together, Wishing Star-Mars, Aspen-Golden, and Golden-Mars form the major 500 kV reinforcement loop around eastern Loudoun.

## 6. Later projects show the next wave of buildout

### BECO-Firehouse

Dominion filed the application in May 2026. The project would add two 230 kV lines to a new Firehouse Substation and is expected to be in service by early 2029.

**TDRI: 60.5**

### Global Plaza Extension

The project is in public outreach, with a target SCC filing in December 2026 and a planned in-service date at the end of 2029.

**TDRI: 43.5**

### Morrisville-Wishing Star

Dominion filed this 500/230 kV reinforcement in February 2026. The project extends bulk-transfer capability toward the Loudoun reliability network.

**TDRI: 56.0**

### Commonwealth Energy Express

The proposed approximately 200-mile HVDC project would connect Heritage Substation in Brunswick County to Mosby Substation in Loudoun County.

V3 treats it as an early-development strategic reinforcement because a public final in-service date is not yet established in the source set.

**TDRI: 34.5**

## 7. What the TDRI means

The Transmission Development Readiness Index is:

- 40% development stage
- 30% regulatory maturity
- 15% schedule specificity
- 15% explicit load linkage

It is a **documented-maturity index**.

It is not:

- available MW;
- spare transformer capacity;
- N-1 deliverability;
- queue priority;
- interconnection cost;
- or a guaranteed energization date.

## 8. V4 requirement

The next research layer should add actual candidate-site evidence:

- serving transmission zone;
- candidate point of interconnection;
- substation / switching-station relationship;
- public transmission constraints;
- planned network upgrades;
- service voltage;
- customer-funded facility requirements;
- land and route constructibility;
- permitting status;
- phased-load profile.

That is the point where Speed-to-Power can begin comparing candidate development areas instead of only infrastructure projects.
