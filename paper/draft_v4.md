# Speed-to-Power V4: Candidate Development Pathways in Northern Virginia

## Working Paper Draft

**September 21, 2026**

## Abstract

This paper extends the Speed-to-Power framework from regional markets, utility commercial structures, and transmission-project maturity to candidate development-pathway evidence. Northern Virginia provides an unusually rich public record in which Dominion Energy Virginia has disclosed requested campus loads, planned load-serving substations, target service dates, bridging-power arrangements, and large-load queue rules.

V4 uses three anonymous but publicly documented campus precedents in eastern Loudoun County: Campus A served through Twin Creeks, Campus B served through Sycolin Creek and Starlight, and Campus C served through Lunar and Apollo. The documented ten-year load requests are 300 MW, 555 MW, and 517 MW respectively. These records are combined with the public 300 MW cap per Dominion delivery-point request and with the maturity of the Twin Creeks-Apollo 230 kV transmission project.

The paper also evaluates BECO-Firehouse and Global Plaza as future transmission-corridor evidence, but deliberately does not assign them candidate-site scores because the public source set does not identify a specific hypothetical site's requested megawatts, delivery-point request, or serving-substation arrangement.

A second methodological change is equally important. Loudoun County's land-use rules now create a separate development gate. Data centers in several industrial districts require Special Exception approval, and in September 2026 the Board of Supervisors approved a plan to pause final votes on legislative data-center and substation applications while policy revisions continue. V4 therefore separates technical power-pathway evidence from land-use entitlement rather than blending them into one number.

## 1. Research question

How can public transmission, queue, campus-load, and land-use evidence be used to compare development pathways for 100–500 MW data-center scenarios without claiming non-public grid capacity?

## 2. Case-study pathways

### Campus A: Twin Creeks

The public transmission filing identifies:

- 300 MW requested ten-year load
- one load-serving substation
- a June 2026 filing-era target
- 30 MVA of bridging capability from Pleasant View

Campus A is therefore a direct precedent for a 300 MW, single-delivery-point architecture.

### Campus B: Sycolin Creek and Starlight

The public filing identifies:

- 300 MW at Sycolin Creek
- 255 MW at Starlight
- 555 MW total
- two delivery points
- a staged target from 2026 through 2028
- no bridging power for Customer B

Campus B is a public precedent for a campus exceeding the 300 MW single-delivery-point limit.

### Campus C: Lunar and Apollo

The public filing identifies:

- 278 MW at Lunar
- 239 MW at Apollo
- 517 MW total
- two delivery points
- 2028 filing-era service targets
- limited bridging capability referenced from Edwards Ferry

Campus C is another public precedent for a 500 MW-scale campus architecture.

## 3. Scenario logic

Dominion's large-load standards cap each delivery-point request at 300 MW.

Accordingly:

| Scenario | Minimum delivery points |
|---:|---:|
| 100 MW | 1 |
| 300 MW | 1 |
| 500 MW | 2 |

A candidate precedent must therefore be evaluated not just by total documented MW but by its delivery-point architecture.

## 4. Technical Pathway Evidence Score

V4 defines:

[
TPES =
0.30L +
0.15D +
0.25T +
0.20S +
0.05B +
0.05E
]

Where:

- (L): documented load fit
- (D): delivery-point fit
- (T): direct transmission-development maturity
- (S): schedule alignment
- (B): bridging-power ratio
- (E): evidence completeness

The score is calculated only when public load and delivery-point evidence exist.

Corridor-only projects remain unscored.

## 5. Why bridging power receives a small weight

Temporary bridging capability can be useful for commissioning or early-stage operations, but the public examples are small relative to ultimate hyperscale demand.

A 30 MVA bridging reference should not materially outweigh the permanent transmission solution for a 300–500 MW campus.

V4 therefore gives bridging only 5% of the total score and scales it against the scenario MW.

## 6. Future corridor evidence

### BECO-Firehouse

The project adds two 230 kV lines between BECO and a new Firehouse Substation. Dominion filed the SCC application in May 2026 and expects the facilities to be in service by early 2029.

The source demonstrates an emerging load-serving corridor but does not establish the MW request or delivery-point architecture of a hypothetical new site.

### Global Plaza

The proposed double-circuit 230 kV extension connects existing lines and requires no new substation under the current project scope. Dominion targets service by the end of 2029.

This is regional reinforcement evidence rather than a complete site power pathway.

## 7. Land-use gate

The power pathway cannot be analyzed independently of Loudoun County's development rules.

### 7.1 Special Exception requirement

On March 18, 2025, Loudoun County approved changes making data centers conditional uses requiring Special Exception approval in Industrial Park, General Industry, and Mineral Resources-Heavy Industry districts, subject to applicable grandfathering rules.

### 7.2 September 2026 pause plan

On September 15, 2026, the Board approved a plan to pause final votes on legislative data-center and electrical-substation applications for up to 12 months.

As of September 21, the implementing resolution was scheduled for October 20, 2026.

The county stated that administrative applications would not be affected.

V4 therefore treats the pause as an elevated entitlement-timing risk, not as a final parcel-specific denial.

### 7.3 Ongoing standards work

Data Center Standards & Locations Phase 2 continues through 2027.

The county is also conducting Phase 2 of its Electrical Infrastructure Comprehensive Plan Amendment to identify potential future high-voltage areas, zones, and routes.

These processes can change the development environment before a multi-year data-center project reaches construction.

## 8. Implication for site screening

A candidate site should pass two separate gates.

### Gate 1: Technical pathway

- requested load
- number of delivery points
- serving substations
- direct transmission dependencies
- target service dates
- bridging options
- queue-stage requirements

### Gate 2: Development entitlement

- current zoning
- Special Exception requirement
- grandfathering status
- land control
- substation entitlement path
- legislative versus administrative processing
- current county policy timing

Passing one gate does not imply passing the other.

## 9. Current limitation

The model does not infer private or non-public system conditions.

It cannot determine:

- spare MW at a substation
- N-1 deliverability
- transformer availability
- final network-upgrade scope
- queue position
- customer contribution
- or guaranteed service timing

A real site decision requires utility engineering studies and parcel-level land-use diligence.

## 10. Conclusion

V4 demonstrates that public evidence can meaningfully narrow the path-to-power problem without pretending to know hidden grid capacity.

The three public campus precedents show how 300–555 MW developments can be decomposed into delivery points, substations, staged service dates, and transmission dependencies.

The Loudoun policy environment shows why technical readiness must remain separate from entitlement readiness.

A credible site-screening model must answer both questions before treating a location as development-ready.
