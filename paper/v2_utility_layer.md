# V2 Research Note: Utility-Level Speed-to-Power

## Scope

Version 2 moves the Speed-to-Power framework from regional transmission organizations to the utility/service-territory level.

The initial sample contains Dominion Energy Virginia, Oncor Electric Delivery, and Commonwealth Edison.

## Core methodological change

A single electricity-cost measure is not valid across the three territories.

Dominion operates as a vertically integrated regulated utility for default service, while Oncor is a transmission and distribution utility in ERCOT's competitive retail market. ComEd provides delivery service in Illinois with competitive supply options.

Accordingly, V2 separates:

- delivery charges
- retail/wholesale energy procurement
- large-load contractual obligations
- collateral
- minimum demand floors
- project-specific infrastructure contributions

## Dominion GS-5

The Virginia State Corporation Commission approved GS-5 for customers with at least 25 MW of measured or contracted demand on a contiguous site and at least a 75% measured or expected load factor, effective January 1, 2027.

The approved structure includes a 14-year contract, an optional ramp period of up to four years with a minimum annual ramp of 20%, collateral of $1.5 million per MW subject to up to a 70% reduction based on credit, and minimum demand charges based on 85% of contracted distribution and transmission demand and 60% of contracted generation demand.

For a hypothetical 300 MW facility, the gross collateral benchmark is $450 million and the maximum credit reduction would reduce that benchmark to $135 million.

## Oncor transmission-voltage service

Oncor's tariff separates delivery service from retail electricity supply.

The V2 model uses the June/August 2026 published transmission-service components and makes ERCOT 4CP exposure an explicit assumption.

This converts a difficult-to-see tariff structure into a controllable scenario variable.

## ComEd high-voltage service

ComEd's published 2026 delivery-service informational sheets define explicit High Voltage Delivery Class charges. For a >10 MW load at or above 69 kV, V2 models the unadjusted customer, metering, high-voltage facilities, transformer, and IEDT components.

Because the published schedule states many components as rate plus ADJ, V2 does not represent its result as a complete bill.

## Contribution

V2 demonstrates that "speed to power" cannot be reduced to an RTO score or average industrial electricity price.

A credible hyperscale screening framework must distinguish the commercial architecture of power service from the physical ability to connect the load.

That distinction is the main contribution of Version 2.
